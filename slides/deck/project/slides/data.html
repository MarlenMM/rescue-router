Data, weights and training

Data

FloodNet (Hurricane Harvey, 2017): 1, 5, 10, 20 or 50 labelled photos to adapt; 30 to tune; 448 to test.

RescueNet (Hurricane Michael, 2018): a second disaster, 20 or 50 photos.

I labelled nothing: all labels are the datasets’.

Weights

The course’s Chameleon meta-trained checkpoint (Kim et al., ECCV 2024), with its BEiT-v2-Large image encoder.

Fine-tuned: biases, matching temperature, one LayerNorm: 5 MB per model. No other pretrained model.

Training

384-px crops, batch 2, Adam, lr 10⁻³ with cosine decay, fp16; 1,000–2,000 steps; best step on the 30 tuning photos.

7 runs on free Kaggle T4 GPUs, about 17 GPU-hours.

The notebook reruns everything on a fresh T4 in about 30 minutes; its live 50-shot predictions match the stored ones exactly on Kaggle and on 99.99 % of pixels on Colab.

Data: FloodNet, from Hurricane Harvey, for the main experiments — I adapt on 1, 5, 10, 20 or 50 labelled photos, tune on 30, and test on all 448 test photos. RescueNet, from Hurricane Michael, is the second disaster. I did not label anything myself; every label is the dataset's own. The only pretrained weights are the course's Chameleon checkpoint; fine-tuning only touches the biases, the matching temperature and one LayerNorm, so each model is five megabytes. Same recipe for every run, sized to fit a free 16-gigabyte T4: 384-pixel crops, batch 2, Adam with a cosine schedule, half precision. Seven runs, about 17 GPU-hours on Kaggle. And the final notebook reruns on a fresh T4 in about half an hour. On Kaggle, where the stored predictions were made, the live ones are identical; on Colab, 99.99 percent of pixels come out the same.
