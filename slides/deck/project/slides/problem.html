In August 2022 Seoul had 141.5 millimetres of rain in one hour, the most since 1907, and Gangnam's streets became rivers. In July 2023 the Osong underpass flooded in minutes and 14 people died. In the first hours, rescue coordinators need one answer per street and per house: can a truck get there, does it need a boat, or is it cut off? Drones deliver photos fast, but labelling one photo takes about an hour, and a normal segmentation model needs over a thousand labelled photos. So my question was: how far do 50 labelled photos get us, and is that map good enough to route rescue teams? The short answer is on the right: .472 mIoU on the whole FloodNet test set, a router that finds cut-off houses with F1 .759, and when the system says "confident: truck", it is right 98.7 percent of the time. Fully supervised PSPNet gets .848, so this is a fast first map, not a replacement.
