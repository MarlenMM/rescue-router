How I encoded it. Every pixel of a 768 by 576 drone photo gets a five-channel label: clear road, flooded road, flooded building, dry building and water. Everything else — trees, grass, cars — is "other". Chameleon predicts one probability map per channel, and I decode it with a per-channel threshold tuned on 30 validation photos. Inference cuts the photo into six overlapping 384-pixel tiles. I chose the channels for the consumer: a router that works on 4-by-4 pixel cells, sends a truck on clear roads, a boat on water, and walks the last stretch. Things I rejected: all ten FloodNet classes, which spends the few labels on trees; a single "passable" channel, which can't plan a boat; and a cost map, for which no labels exist.
