What I learned. The most dangerous mistake is shallow water read as dry land: the map then invites a truck into water. Here is that failure, honestly: the model thought this flooded street was land. But the six houses it wrongly calls truck-reachable are all flagged "check first", because the system routes every map twice, once cautiously. A simple rule from the failure analysis — no off-road driving where the water probability is high — raised the cut-off F1 from .74 to .84. So: saying how sure we are matters more than a few IoU points. Things I tried and dropped: test-time flips, which looked good on the tuning photos but lost on the test set; more labels between 5 and 10, which gave nothing measurable; and smoothing. Limits: these are US hurricanes, not Korean city floods; single photos without GPS; and the labelled map is my router on human labels, not field-checked access. Thank you.
